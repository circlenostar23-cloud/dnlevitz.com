The problem I had

I was used to keeping up with US news through podcasts. After I moved to Amsterdam I looked for one that would do the same for the Netherlands, and the ones I found were mostly in Dutch or German. I wanted something to listen to in the morning that also explained the background I’m missing: who this minister is, why a court ruling keeps coming up, what a works council does. So I built my own.

How I went about it

I wanted two hosts talking, not one voice reading headlines, so one of them can ask the question I’d ask (“wait, why does the Senate matter here?”) and the other can answer it. Once it was running, I tuned the mix: politics capped at about three stories, airtime split by how much each story has to say, a guaranteed place for big Amsterdam news and two or three lighter stories to end on.

How it works

These steps run on GitHub Actions early every morning. A tag under a step leads to the page about it.

  1. CollectNews feeds from five outlets (NOS, NU.nl, Het Parool, NL Times, DutchNews) and what the past week’s episodes already covered.Memory→
  2. SelectAn AI model picks 6 to 9 stories and marks each as hard news or light.
  3. BriefIt writes an English briefing from the full articles and lists each story’s open questions.
  4. CheckA fact-checker looks up each story’s open questions, with every story checked at the same time. If a lookup fails, the story runs as written.Fact-checking→
  5. What’s OnA few things to do in Amsterdam, picked from the city’s cultural calendar.What's On→
  6. Plan and scriptEach story gets a word budget, and then the two-host script is written to fit.
  7. VoiceText-to-speech with both hosts in one recording, free on a normal day. If a part fails, a paid service redoes only that part in the same voices, and free Microsoft voices are the last resort. A cue marks each break between stories.Voices→Sound→
  8. PublishThe audio goes out to a private podcast feed on my phone with a chapter per story, live captions and show notes that link each story’s sources. The episode is also added to the episode list on this site.Show notes→

How it’s worked out

It has put out an episode every day since launch, and I listen to it every morning. Almost none of the code is specific to Dutch news, so I moved it into a shared engine that can run more than one show. A friend wanted a daily briefing on New York theater, film and art, built around the Broadway Briefing newsletter, and development of that show is going smoothly on the foundation I built here.
