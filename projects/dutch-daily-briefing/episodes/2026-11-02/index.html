Airbnb's Data Handover, a Broken Promise to Sick Children, and a Phantom Train

New holiday rental rules mean Airbnb and Booking.com now have to hand host data to the government, and Amsterdam is ready to use it. Care homes for seriously ill children say they'll close by year's end even though about 25 children still have nowhere suitable to go. Plus a warning about elderly alarm buttons, Verstappen's win in Mexico, a ghost on the Amsterdam metro, and a wolf called Bram.

Monday, 2 November 2026 · 14 min

Today's headlines

0:0014:08
Chapters

Read more

  • Holiday rentals: platforms must share host data: Since 1 November, Airbnb and Booking.com must send monthly host data that 34 registering municipalities can use for enforcement. (NU.nl)
  • Care homes for seriously ill children to close: Villa ExpertCare will shut its last two homes at the end of 2026, though about 25 children still lack places. (NL Times)
  • Alarm buttons and the 2G switch-off: Up to 80,000 personal alarms still rely on 2G, and KPN switches it off on 1 December 2027. (NU.nl)
  • Antonelli champion, Verstappen wins in Mexico: Mercedes’ 20-year-old Kimi Antonelli became F1’s youngest champion, while Verstappen won from fifth on the grid. (NL Times, NOS)
  • A phantom train behind the metro shutdown: Amsterdam’s metro stopped for hours after a train that wasn’t there was detected, which GVB traces to botched maintenance. (NL Times)
  • Recalled cars still on the road: Over 400,000 Dutch cars have serious unrepaired recalls, including about 160,000 with dangerous Takata airbags. (NOS)
  • Twente’s record nine-nil: FC Twente won 9-0 at ten-man Excelsior, its biggest Eredivisie win, with Wout Weghorst scoring four. (NL Times, NOS)
  • Bram the problem wolf goes to the museum: Bram, the wolf shot after biting people on the Utrechtse Heuvelrug, is now stuffed and on display at Naturalis. (NOS, NU.nl)
