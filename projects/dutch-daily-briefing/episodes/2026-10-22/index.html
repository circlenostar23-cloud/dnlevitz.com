The Day the Dutch Internet Switched Itself Off

Two critical flaws in PHP led DigiD, MijnOverheid, parliament and news sites to go offline as a precaution. We look at what happened and what's still unknown. Plus the first health insurance premium for 2027, a truce in the regional transport strike, the latest in the Hajar investigation, MPs reading out AI-written speeches, and Channel smuggling boats coming through the Netherlands. To finish: Formula 1's new home, a lost Erasmus book, a train's second farewell and the weekend in Amsterdam.

  • PHP flaws take DigiD, MijnOverheid and parliament offline: Two critical PHP flaws, dubbed PHP2Shell, let attackers run code on servers without logging in, prompting precautionary shutdowns. (NL Times, DutchNews.nl)
  • DSW announces record premium rise for 2027: DSW’s basic premium rises €14.75 to €173.25 a month, its biggest euro increase ever. (NOS, NU.nl)
  • Regional transport strike called off as talks resume: FNV and CNV suspended action for two weeks after employers withdrew proposals to worsen the collective agreement. (NL Times, NOS)
  • Hajar likely killed in Buitenveldert anti-squat building: Police think 29-year-old Hajar died in a former office building; 23-year-old suspect Abdi A. is held under full restrictions. (NL Times, DutchNews.nl)
  • Nearly half of MPs read out AI-written speeches: An investigation found 68 of 150 MPs gave speeches this year that were mostly written with tools like ChatGPT. (DutchNews.nl, NOS)
  • Customs to check imports of Channel smuggling boats: Inflatable boats are arriving more often on cargo flights from Turkey, sold in smuggling packages worth €10,000 to €20,000. (NOS)
  • Formula 1 and darts move from Viaplay to Videoland: Regulator ACM approved Videoland’s €142 million takeover of Viaplay Netherlands’ sports rights and subscribers. (NOS)
  • A ‘lost’ Erasmus book on manners returns to Rotterdam: Rotterdam’s library bought the only known copy of a 1609 French edition of Erasmus’s children’s manners book. (NOS)
  • The ‘Apekop’ train’s second farewell: The Mat ’64 ran its last regular Valleilijn service on Wednesday and returns to its foundation for enthusiast trips. (NOS)
