Moerdijk Might Be Saved, Amsterdam Tackles Crack, and the Slow Train to Berlin

The cabinet now wants to keep Moerdijk, the Brabant village that was set to be demolished for port expansion, but its residents face another year and a half of waiting. We also cover Amsterdam's €6 million plan against drug nuisance, the Senate's narrow vote on research embryos, a new Dutch-German rail deal, a damning report on animal welfare, and a rise in international students. Plus an Olympic champion who now swims for the Netherlands, and bears replacing the Bijenkorf's climbing Pieten.

Read more

  • Moerdijk village may be saved: The cabinet now aims to keep the village because the planned port expansion needs about 200 hectares, not 400 to 500. (DutchNews.nl, NOS)
  • Amsterdam’s offensive against drug nuisance: The city’s spending €6 million on crack-related nuisance, including 274 extra shelter beds and night shelters with drug-use rooms. (NOS, Het Parool)
  • Senate approves research embryos: In a 38–35 vote, scientists can now create embryos for hereditary disease research, kept no longer than 14 days. (NL Times, DutchNews.nl)
  • A Dutch-German rail deal: The Netherlands and Germany have pledged more cross-border trains and shorter journeys, but set no target times and committed no money. (NOS, NU.nl)
  • Animal welfare report and the Beter Leven label: A ministry committee finds the meat sector’s welfare system is failing, with farmers, slaughterhouses, the NVWA and the ministry all to blame. (DutchNews.nl, NOS)
  • International students are back, with a ‘Trump effect’: About 17,600 new international students enrolled at Dutch universities, up almost 6 percent, partly because of developments in the US. (DutchNews.nl, NOS)
  • An Olympic champion swims for the Netherlands: Two-time Olympic breaststroke champion Tatjana Smith is switching from South Africa and will debut at December’s short-course worlds in Beijing. (NOS)
  • Bears replace the Bijenkorf’s climbing Pieten: After decades of climbing Piet figures dating back to 1963, de Bijenkorf’s using its mascot Bob de Beer in Piet costume. (NU.nl, Het Parool)
