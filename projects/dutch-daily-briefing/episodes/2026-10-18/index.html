France Burns, a Leiden Clinic's Final Count, and a Ring Lost for 83 Years

Cost-of-living protests turned violent across France and Belgium, with more than 1,200 arrests and Limoges' historic city hall destroyed by fire. At home, a final report says a Leiden fertility-clinic lab technician fathered at least 16 children, Amsterdam police seized an activist server at America's request, and poverty relief groups are starting waiting lists. Plus a goodbye to TV favourite Maarten Spanjer, marathon day in Amsterdam, and a wedding ring that went home after 83 years.

  • Leiden donor fraud: the count rises to sixteen: A clinic’s final report says a lab technician fathered 16 donor children, not 11, and carried two genetic conditions. (NOS)
  • A server seized for the US, and a nervous cloud industry: Amsterdam police seized a server belonging to Italian activist provider A/I after the US labeled it a terrorist group. (NL Times, NU.nl)
  • France and Belgium: protests turn violent, Limoges city hall burns: About 550,000 people joined a month-old pupil movement in protests across France, and some marches turned violent. (NOS, NU.nl)
  • Poverty relief groups start waiting lists: Two in three local aid groups say requests for help rose over the past year, and 11 percent now keep waiting lists. (NL Times)
  • Overbidding on homes is cooling: Nearly two-thirds of homes went above asking price last quarter, down from 71 percent, as listings rose 24 percent. (NU.nl)
  • More street preachers, more complaints: Nine of 18 municipalities say street preaching is rising, and Zwolle’s mayor wants the law changed to deal with complaints. (NL Times, NOS)
  • Farewell to Maarten Spanjer: The actor and presenter, best known for the hidden-camera show Taxi, has died of cancer at 73. (NOS, NU.nl)
  • Amsterdam Marathon day: Dutch champion Mikky Keetels, a KLM flight attendant, aims to break 2:30 on Sunday as both defending champions return. (NU.nl, Het Parool)
  • A wedding ring comes home after 83 years: A metal detectorist found the ring Jo Nabuurs lost in 1942–43 and returned it to his son Bert. (NOS)
