Albert Heijn Restarts, Asylum Rules Pass, and Poems on Garbage Trucks

Albert Heijn is slowly restocking after a cyberattack routed through servers in four countries, and there's still no sign that customer data was stolen. We also cover the Senate's new asylum measures, an urgent warning from the domestic-abuse agency Veilig Thuis, rising fuel and gas prices, today's regional transport strike and a bigger mortgage guarantee. Then it's Russian gymnasts refused visas for Rotterdam, poetry on Rotterdam's garbage trucks becoming national heritage, and ideas for tonight in Amsterdam.

Read more

  • Albert Heijn restarts after the cyberattack: Ahold Delhaize has restarted some systems and is restocking stores from several distribution centres, but deliveries are improvised for now. (DutchNews.nl, NOS)
  • Senate passes asylum measures: Senators backed ending IND penalty payments and easier deportation for asylum seekers convicted of offences carrying two-plus years. (NOS, NU.nl)
  • Veilig Thuis says it can’t cope: The domestic violence hotline wants €70 million a year, as half of reports miss the five-day deadline and waits average two months. (DutchNews.nl, NOS)
  • Inflation, fuel and gas prices: Prices rose 3.4 percent in September, driven by fuel up 27.2 percent, while Euro95’s recommended price hit a record €2.732. (DutchNews.nl, NU.nl)
  • Regional transport strike: Bus and train drivers at Arriva, Qbuzz, Transdev and others begin weekly walkouts over pay, while NS runs normally. (NOS)
  • Mortgage guarantee limit rises: From 2027 the national mortgage guarantee will cover loans up to €500,000, or €530,000 with energy-saving measures. (NU.nl, NOS)
  • Pride arson case: Prosecutors say Anes A., 27, acted with terrorist intent when he set fires on a train and at Amsterdam Centraal. (NL Times, Het Parool)
  • Russian gymnasts refused Dutch visas: World champion Angelina Melnikova and two teammates can’t compete in Rotterdam’s world championships, an Olympic qualifier, says their federation. (NL Times, NOS)
  • Poetry on Rotterdam’s garbage trucks: Rotterdam’s tradition of poetry lines on garbage trucks, started in 1988, is now recognised as Dutch intangible cultural heritage. (NU.nl)
