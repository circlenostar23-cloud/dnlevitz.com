The Student Loan Promise That Doesn't Add Up

The education minister says the coalition's 2.5 percent cap on student loan interest would cost at least four times the money set aside, and student groups are furious. We also have new details in the killing of Hajar in Amsterdam, the closure of the Dutch embassy in Cairo, a legal fight over Schiphol and a crackdown on deadly fake-pill webshops. Plus a record night for Dutch football, Airbnb's new checkout rules, a whistling tower in Rotterdam and ideas for your weekend in Amsterdam.

Friday, 23 October 2026 · 15 min

  • The student loan cap falls apart: Minister Letschert says a 2.5% student loan interest cap would cost at least €160 million a year, not €40 million. (NOS, NU.nl)
  • Hajar: a plea for help, and a disputed wait: Police confirm Hajar messaged for help the morning she vanished, but they judged her case non-urgent that day. (NL Times, NOS)
  • Dutch embassy in Cairo closes: After nine days of protests over Ahmed Nasser’s detention in the Netherlands, the embassy’s shut to the public indefinitely. (NOS)
  • Schiphol’s new decree heads to court: Green groups and residents are asking the Council of State to annul a decree allowing up to 500,000 flights a year. (NU.nl)
  • Fake-pill webshops taken down: Police shut 30 sites and arrested three men over fake pills linked to at least three women’s deaths. (DutchNews.nl, NOS)
  • National Slavery Museum delayed to 2033: Amsterdam’s slavery museum will open three years late, and construction costs are now put at over €110 million. (Het Parool, NU.nl)
  • A historic night for Dutch football: AZ, NEC, Twente and Ajax all won in Europe on Thursday, which hadn’t happened since February 2012. (NL Times, NOS)
  • Airbnb hosts can’t make you do the dishes: From 20 November, hosts can’t make guests strip beds, wash dishes or take out rubbish, though reasonable requests are allowed. (DutchNews.nl, NOS)
  • The Euromast’s mystery whistle: Residents have filed hundreds of complaints about a wind-dependent noise from the Rotterdam tower that continued after cables were removed. (NOS)

What’s on in Amsterdam (Friday 23 to Sunday 25 October 2026)

  • Post-Space: sound and light in the Old Church: Minimal Collective presents live electronic sets, DJs and an audiovisual installation in Amsterdam’s oldest church during ADE. (Friday 23 and Saturday 24 October, evening programmes; installation also open Friday and Saturday during regular church hours, Oude Kerk (Old Church), city centre)
  • Carel Visser’s iron sculptures in the Rijksmuseum gardens: A free outdoor exhibition brings together large welded-steel sculptures by Carel Visser that usually stand in Dutch cities. (Friday 23 to Sunday 25 October, 09:00–18:00, Rijksmuseum gardens, Oud-Zuid)
  • Signals: Hearing a Changing Planet: Artists turn ESA climate satellite data into sound and images, followed by a panel with an ESA scientist. (Sunday 25 October, 19:30–21:30, ARTIS Planetarium, ARTIS Amsterdam Royal Zoo, Plantage)
  • The Maker Market: Independent makers sell jewellery, ceramics, illustrations and sustainable goods at De Hallen, a former tram depot. (Saturday 24 October 11:00–17:00 and Sunday 25 October 12:00–17:00, De Hallen, Amsterdam-West)
  • Night of the Night at Fort Island Pampus: Take a boat to a blacked-out island fort, explore it by candlelight with guides and get warm soup. (Saturday 24 October; boat leaves Muiden at 19:00, back at 22:00, Forteiland Pampus, boat from Muiden (just outside Amsterdam))
