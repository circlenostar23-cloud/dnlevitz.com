Seven Parties, One Tax Plan

The Jetten minority cabinet starts talks this week with seven opposition parties to get its tax plan through, and a small print change hits new entrepreneurs. We also cover energy bills that are up about €1,100 in ten months, a cut to drought funding, a panther attack at Beekse Bergen and a second weekend of protests in France. Then the clocks going back, ADE's last day and a tenth straight European korfball title.

Sunday, 25 October 2026 · 13 min

Today's headlines

0:0012:57
Chapters

Read more

  • The cabinet’s hunt for tax plan votes: With just 66 House seats, D66, VVD and CDA will invite seven opposition parties to negotiate their tax plan. (NOS, NU.nl)
  • Energy bills climb as winter arrives: An average household now pays just over €2,700 a year, about €1,100 more than ten months ago. (NU.nl)
  • Drought money cut for bridges and sluices: The cabinet is cutting €284 million from drought prevention to help fund €18 billion of infrastructure repairs. (NU.nl)
  • Keeper seriously hurt in panther attack: A Beekse Bergen keeper of 25 years was badly injured by Persian leopards kept indoors during storm Austen. (NOS, NU.nl)
  • France’s second protest day, and unrest across Europe: About 410,000 people marched in France on Saturday and police arrested almost 1,000, with 70,000 extra officers deployed. (NOS, NU.nl)
  • ADE’s last day: Around 600,000 visitors came to Amsterdam Dance Event this year, matching the record, though hotels charged steep prices. (NL Times, NOS)
  • The clocks went back: Winter time is back until 28 March 2027, and EU countries still can’t agree on ending the switch. (DutchNews.nl, NOS)
  • Korfball: ten out of ten: The Netherlands beat Belgium 27-12 in the final and have now won all ten European titles since 1998. (NOS)
