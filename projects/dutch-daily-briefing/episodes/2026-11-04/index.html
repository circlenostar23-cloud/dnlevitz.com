Tulips Spared, Votes Still Missing: The Revised Tax Plan

The cabinet's revised tax plan pushes back the new box 3 tax, puts the flower VAT rise on hold and drops a planned labour tax, but it still doesn't have a majority. We also cover the new nitrogen law, Democrats taking the US House, big pension rises for two million retirees, a shrinking rental market, two Amsterdam restaurants shut over suspected exploitation, and the Amsterdam Museum turning 100.

  • Revised tax plan: box 3 delayed, flower VAT on hold, but no majority yet: The cabinet now aims for box 3 by 2029 and has shelved a flower VAT rise to 21 percent, costing €76 million. (DutchNews.nl, NOS)
  • Nitrogen law gives provinces more say: The law sets 2035 emission cuts of 42 to 46 percent for farming, with €9 billion for affected farmers. (NOS, NU.nl)
  • US midterms: Democrats take the House, Senate still open: Projections give Democrats at least 244 House seats; they’ve flipped two Senate seats but need a net gain of four. (NOS, NU.nl)
  • Pension rises of 12 to 25 percent for two million retirees: Aon says 37 funds switching systems on 1 January are healthy, with retirees at the ten largest gaining 17 percent on average. (DutchNews.nl, NOS)
  • Private rentals shrink as starter loans run dry: Private rentals fell by 22,000 to 1.16 million in 2025, while rising house prices drain councils’ starter-loan budgets. (NL Times, NU.nl)
  • Parliament majority wants a year-round off-peak pass: The motion would follow the €49-a-month summer pass, which about 365,000 people bought; D66 and CDA back it. (NL Times, NU.nl)
  • Two Amsterdam restaurants closed over suspected exploitation: Mayor Halsema shut Kailash Parbat and Anjappar for three months; some staff reportedly worked 60 to 80 hours a week. (Het Parool)
  • Rico Verhoeven’s fight with Wilder is off: The planned 11 December Cardiff bout fell through after talks stalled, and Verhoeven won’t fight again this year. (NU.nl)
  • The Amsterdam Museum turns 100, with walks to match: A new book links 100 museum objects to twelve city walks, part of a 100-day festival running until 11 December. (Het Parool)
