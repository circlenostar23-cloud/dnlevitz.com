The Riots That Weren't, and a Tax Plan Still Stuck

Saturday's feared 'Gen Z' riots mostly didn't happen. Police in The Hague arrested 34 people, most of them teenagers, and prosecutors want triple sentences. Weekend talks at the Catshuis ended without a tax-plan deal, which sets up a showdown in parliament on Wednesday. Also today: a first-ever migrant boat rescue off Zeeland, a missing Peter Greenaway film paid for partly with Dutch money, a professor's AI twin, and the bats that are throwing off your rain app.

  • Feared ‘Gen Z’ riots fizzle: Police in The Hague arrested 34 people, mostly aged 15 to 18, and prosecutors want triple the usual sentences. (NL Times, NOS)
  • No tax deal at the Catshuis: The cabinet still lacks a majority for its tax plan, and the House votes on 12 November. (NOS, NU.nl)
  • Migrant boat rescued off Zeeland: Thirty-eight men without life jackets were rescued from an engineless rubber boat drifting near Breskens on Saturday. (NL Times, NU.nl)
  • Delayed road projects mean more traffic jams: The construction institute EIB estimates that shelving 22 road projects will add 16 million hours of congestion a year. (NL Times)
  • Amsterdam’s homeless office closed for weeks: Staff absences closed the walk-in office in Amsterdam-West in early October, and it hopes to reopen on 2 November. (Het Parool)
  • The Film Fund and the film that never came out: Nieuwsuur and NRC found the fund paid over 550,000 euros to Peter Greenaway’s Lucca Mortis without required conditions being proven. (NOS)
  • A professor’s AI digital twin: Jan Rotmans, who has metastatic prostate cancer, is having an avatar built to give lectures after he dies. (NOS)
  • Bats versus the rain radar: Poplars blocking the KNMI radar at Herwijnen are spoiling rain forecasts, but West Betuwe won’t let them be pruned yet. (NOS)
