76 to 74: The Tax Plan Passes, and the VVD Has to Decide

The Jetten cabinet's 2027 tax plan scraped through the lower house without its own coalition partner, the VVD, and now the government's future hangs on what the VVD decides. We also look at nearly a million households facing higher mortgage payments, steeper health insurance premiums, Amsterdam's tourist tax hike and its council's Schiphol rebellion, and Jensen Huang's exit from Nvidia. Plus Maloe Melo, Wesley Sneijder's surprising first coaching job, a record-breaking rebel coin, and what's on in Amsterdam this weekend.

  • Tax plan passes without the VVD: MPs passed the 2027 plan 76–74 after the VVD voted against it over a wealth-tax amendment. (DutchNews.nl, NL Times)
  • Mortgage payments set to rise: DNB says about 950,000 households face higher mortgage payments by 2028, typically around €150 more a month. (DutchNews.nl, NOS)
  • Health insurance premiums jump: CZ, VGZ and Menzis are raising basic premiums by more than the €12.50 a month the ministry expected. (NL Times, NU.nl)

  • Council sends city back to court over Schiphol: Councillors overruled the city government, telling it to appeal the new decree allowing 478,000 Schiphol flights a year. (Het Parool)
  • Nvidia’s Jensen Huang steps down: Nvidia’s co-founder quit as CEO after an affair with an alleged Chinese spy came out, and CFO Colette Kress steps in. (NOS, NU.nl)
  • Maloe Melo’s owner forced to stop: Jur Scherpenzeel, 83, has had to give up the Amsterdam blues café he’s run since 1984. (Het Parool)
  • Wesley Sneijder’s first coaching job: The Netherlands’ most-capped player, 42, is joining Utrecht amateur club Eminent Boys, where his brother plays. (NU.nl)
  • Record price for a rebel gold coin: A 1580 Frisian gold half Philipsdaalder sold for €369,000, a record for a Dutch provincial coin. (DutchNews.nl, NL Times)
