A Blue Sweep, an Open Marriage and HEMA at 100

Democrats win the US Senate as well as the House, and Trump says he accepts the result. In The Hague, the coalition lets its own parties vote different ways on the tax plan. Also today: inflation climbs again, Klaas Knot is nominated to run the European Central Bank, human rights groups pull back from the International Criminal Court, a new study on rental discrimination, HEMA turns 100, and 1.7 million fake Pokémon cards.

  • Inflation rises to 3.7 percent: Dutch prices rose 3.7% in October, the highest since last September, with energy up 14.4% on a year ago. (NOS, NU.nl)
  • Democrats sweep Congress: Democrats flipped seven Republican seats to hold at least 54 of 100, with Mississippi and Alaska still uncalled. (DutchNews.nl, NOS)
  • The coalition’s ‘open marriage’ on taxes: D66, VVD and CDA may vote separately on the tax plan as a last resort, testing a Pro-Volt wealth-tax proposal. (NU.nl, NOS)
  • Klaas Knot nominated to lead the ECB: The Dutch government wants former central bank chief Knot to replace Lagarde, who leaves early on 7 February. (DutchNews.nl, NOS)
  • Human rights groups pull back from the ICC: At least twenty groups, including Human Rights Watch, scaled back or ended cooperation, many because of US sanctions. (NU.nl, NOS)
  • Your name and your chances of renting: A test with 4,096 fake applications found Moroccan-sounding names got fewer viewing invites, reversing the last monitor’s finding. (Het Parool)
  • Marco Borsato wants his legal costs back: Acquitted last year, the singer is asking the state for over €431,000, mostly for his lawyers. (NOS, NU.nl)
  • HEMA turns 100: The chain opened on Amsterdam’s Kalverstraat in 1926 selling fixed-price goods and now has over 700 stores. (NOS)
  • 1.7 million fake Pokémon cards: Rotterdam customs seized 1,719,000 counterfeit cards from a Maasvlakte container in May; Nintendo confirmed they were fake. (NOS)
