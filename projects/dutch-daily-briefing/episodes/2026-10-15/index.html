Albert Heijn Restocks, Police Want a Complaint, and Your IND Permit

Albert Heijn's distribution centres are running again, and police and prosecutors are publicly urging its parent company to report the cyberattack. A new law also ends IND late-decision payouts for skilled migrants, students and family members, and ASML says it can't build chip machines fast enough. Also today: unrest over a bonfire in Amsterdam-Noord, a US-requested server seizure, the plan to phase out dolphins at the Dolfinarium, a White House loan to the Van Gogh Museum, the fight over the real Limburgse vlaai, and what's on in Amsterdam this weekend.

  • Albert Heijn: shelves refilling, police want a complaint: All distribution centres are running again, but online ordering, the app and customer service remain down with no return date. (NU.nl, NOS)
  • ASML’s record quarter: The chip-machine maker made nearly €5 billion net profit on €11.7 billion sales, with customers already ordering for 2029. (DutchNews.nl, NOS)
  • IND payouts end for skilled migrants, students and families: A planned law means late IND decisions on regular residence permits will no longer earn applicants daily court-ordered penalty payments. (DutchNews.nl)
  • Riot police in Floradorp over a bonfire: Residents in Amsterdam-Noord threw stones and fireworks at firefighters after Mayor Halsema rejected their New Year’s bonfire plan; three were arrested. (NOS, NU.nl)
  • Starter loans running dry: Advisers say rising house prices mean first-time buyers now need €30,000–40,000 loans, and Goes has already exhausted its 2026 budget. (NU.nl)
  • Amsterdam police seize a server for the U.S.: The server belongs to Italian activist collective Autistici/Inventati, which Washington designated a terrorist organisation in August. (NL Times, Het Parool)
  • A breeding ban for dolphins: The cabinet wants it within two years to phase out captive dolphins, while the 22 in Harderwijk live out their lives. (DutchNews.nl, NU.nl)
  • The White House lends Amsterdam a Whistler: Whistler’s ‘Nocturne’ joins ‘Whistler’s Mother’ in the Van Gogh Museum’s 100-work show, the first Whistler retrospective in the Netherlands. (NL Times)
  • Limburg defends the real vlaai: The province is paying up to €100,000 for a bakers’ campaign explaining the vlaai’s EU-protected status and baking rules. (NU.nl)
