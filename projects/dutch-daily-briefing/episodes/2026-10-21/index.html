An arrest in the Hajar case, pricier trains, and a minister under fire over abuse at a home in Spain

Amsterdam police have arrested a 23-year-old man over the killing of Hajar. NS says train fares will rise 4.7 percent next year, and the youth minister is refusing an independent inquiry into a Spanish care home where Dutch children say they were abused. Also today: takeaway vouchers, PostNL's money troubles, churches offering shelter to refugees, Gerard Joling leaving De Toppers, ADE turning thirty and what's on in Amsterdam tonight.

Wednesday, 21 October 2026 · 13 min

Read more

  • Train fares rise again: NS is raising fares by an average of 4.7% in January 2027, above the 2.7% inflation forecast. (DutchNews.nl, NOS)
  • Arrest in the killing of Hajar: Police have arrested a 23-year-old man seen cycling with her before her body was found in the Amstel. (NL Times, NOS)
  • Abuse at a Dutch-run care home in Spain: Sixteen former residents told Nieuwsuur about abuse at Huize Soria, a home near Alicante paid with Dutch care money. (NOS)
  • Takeaway apps stop hiding fees: Thuisbezorgd and Uber Eats will show service and packaging fees upfront after pressure from the Consumentenbond. (DutchNews.nl, NL Times)
  • PostNL wants more money, and a village goes without mail: PostNL is asking the government for an extra €80 million for 2027, saying mail volumes have fallen 70% in 20 years. (NOS, NU.nl)
  • Churches offer shelter to refugees with permits: Dutch churches are being asked to house refugee families with permits for three months, freeing up asylum centre beds. (DutchNews.nl, NOS)
  • A 20km/h trial for Amsterdam cyclists: There’s a 20km/h cycling limit on 300 metres of the Amstelveenseweg until 13 November, but nobody will be fined. (DutchNews.nl)
  • Gerard Joling leaves De Toppers: After more than 21 years, the 66-year-old singer is quitting to focus on TV and his farewell shows at Ahoy. (NOS, NU.nl)
  • ADE turns thirty: More than 3,600 artists play over 1,300 events from 21 to 25 October, and GVB is running extra night services. (NL Times, Het Parool)

What’s on in Amsterdam (Wednesday 21 October 2026)

  • Heliotrope by Heleen Blanken & Daniel de Bruin: This free installation of turning mirrored slats in Westerpark reflects the park around it and changes from day to night. (Wednesday 21 October, open all day (on until Sunday 25 October), Westerpark, Amsterdam-West)
  • Swan Lake Remixed for ADE: Dutch National Ballet dancers mix ballet and urban dance to new electronic music by Rik Ronner in a former gasholder. (Wednesday 21 October, 19:00–20:00, Gashouder, Westergas, Amsterdam-West)
