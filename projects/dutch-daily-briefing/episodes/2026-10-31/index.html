Boarded Up for 'Gen Z' Saturday, and a Station on the Move

Shops in The Hague board up and police get ready after anonymous online calls for 'Gen Z' protests in several Dutch cities today. We also cover the stalled tax plan talks, a strong economy, possible regional bus strikes, new asylum figures and the courier tasered in Amsterdam. Plus a cinema in an old sewage silo, a rescue for the Pieterburen seals, a 720-tonne station building on the move, and ideas for tonight in Amsterdam.

Saturday, 31 October 2026 · 15 min

Read more

  • ‘Gen Z’ protest calls put Dutch cities on alert: Social media calls for Saturday youth protests, some urging looting, name Amsterdam, The Hague, Maastricht and Eindhoven. (NL Times, DutchNews.nl)
  • Tax plan talks stall on both sides: Friday’s talks brought no deal, with Jetten refusing to give ground on the right-wing bloc’s nitrogen and abortion demands. (DutchNews.nl, NOS)
  • Economy beats expectations: The Dutch economy grew 0.6 percent in the third quarter, with industry, including ASML, driving more than half of it. (NU.nl, NOS)
  • Regional bus strikes could return: FNV and CNV called the employers’ final offer of 2 percent now and 4 percent next year inadequate. (NL Times)
  • Asylum applications drop under new EU rules: First-time asylum applications fell 13 percent to about 5,900 in the third quarter, though they’re still up for the year. (DutchNews.nl, NOS)
  • Tasered courier to file complaint: Police say the courier refused to show ID and grabbed an officer’s wrist, while his lawyer plans a criminal complaint. (NL Times, NOS)
  • Rialto opens a cinema in a sewage silo: Rialto’s new five-screen Zeeburgereiland cinema starts try-out screenings on 3 November and regular films on 5 November. (Het Parool)
  • Pieterburen seals pulled back from the brink: The Lauwersoog seal centre has put its bankruptcy filing on hold after raising 1.3 million euros in three weeks. (NU.nl, NOS)
  • Vught’s station building takes a very slow walk: Vught’s 720-tonne station building from 1868 was moved 13.6 metres east in one piece to make room for a fourth track. (NOS)
