Amsterdam theater planner

A planner I started for the Amsterdam Fringe, to score shows against my taste and fit several into a day near home. It now covers 25 venues all year, and an AI agent keeps the listings up to date.

  • 25venues in one calendar
  • 1–5my interest score per show
  • ≤3 kmcounts as near home
  • Weeklyrefresh by a scheduled agent

The problem I had

This started with the Amsterdam Fringe. In 2026 it ran for eleven days, 3 to 13 September, with more than 50 productions at over 20 venues all over town. Many of them were on the same evenings, and each one played only a few times. I wanted to see several shows a day, so every day had the same two questions: which of these are worth my time and which I can string together without crossing town between them.

The festival site lists shows one at a time. It doesn’t tell you which ones suit your taste or what else is on a short walk away straight after.

How I went about it

I started by asking Claude, and I gave it a profile of what I like so its answers weren’t generic:

What I told it about my taste
Look forCircus, puppetry, shadow play, live music, multimedia, surreal or dark comedy
Fine withDrama and heavy themes
AvoidGory or body-horror performance art
PreferEnsembles over solo shows
TravelUp to about 40 minutes from home

Every show I looked at went on a shortlist with my own interest score from 1 to 5, its dates and venues, and a short summary. When I asked what to see on a given day, it worked from the scores first.

Distance was the other half. Each venue has a distance from home, sorted into bands: up to 3 km is near, up to 7 km is fine, and anything further means checking the route first. That’s what let me plan a day: a high-scoring show, then whatever else was good and close enough to make in time.

How it works

  1. CollectOne small scraper per venue reads the official program into a shared format, for today through the next 120 days.
  2. TagGenre, language and ticket status (sold out, last tickets, cancelled) are read from each listing.
  3. PlaceEach listing carries its venue's distance from home and its band.
  4. ScoreShows I'm considering go on a shortlist with a 1–5 interest score.
  5. PlanWhen I ask about a day, Claude ranks what's on by score and distance against my profile and says which shows are sold out or nearly.

I didn’t build an app or a database. The output is a few Markdown pages and one spreadsheet file, which I can read on my phone and which costs nothing to run.

What I changed after the festival

When the Fringe ended I wanted the same thing for the rest of the year. I pointed the scrapers at the venues the festival had used and then added the big houses like Carré and the Concertgebouw.

The part I expected to kill the project was maintenance. With 25 venue sites, a scraper breaks whenever a venue redesigns its page. So I handed that job to an AI agent.

Every week, without me
  1. RefreshA scheduled Claude session re-reads all 25 venues
  2. ReportIt reads what changed and what broke
  3. FixIt repairs the scrapers that broke
  4. SaveThe new calendar is saved for me

To make that work, a broken venue can’t take the rest down with it. When a site changes, the planner keeps that venue’s last good listings and flags it in a report. The agent starts from that report.
